# A16. Discover 3 local security incidents.

**Overview:** the following three incidents are local Australian cybersecurity events from across multiple sectors, which includes universities, medical research facilities and critical financial infrastructures.

1. **AustralianSuper Credential Stuffing Attack (March 2025)**
    - In March 2025, attackers used stolen credentials from previous unrelated data breaches to launch coordinated credential stuffing attacks, which is an automated technique where large lists of previously leaked usernames and password pairs are systematically tested against login portals, with 5 major Australian superannuation funds targeted. As the AustralianSuper’s web login portal lacked MFA, there was no secondary authentication to block the reuse of credentials.
        
        About 600 accounts were compromised, and 4 members lost around 500,000 in total. About 8,000 members were affected but faced no financial loss due to quick system shutdown. The affected accounts were locked, with the members notified. Due to this incident, AustralianSuper accelerated its MFA rollout for their web login portals.

2. **University of Notre Dame Australia Ransomware Attack (January 2025)**
    - In January 2025, the Fog Ransomware group attacked the University of Notre Dame Australia, stealing around 62.2GB of data, which includes staff records and student documents, before deploying ransomware that disrupted the core university services. This incident was primarily reported through ransomware tracking sources (BlackFog), which aggregates data from ransomware group leak sites.
        
        Students and staff were unable to access online services, with one student reportedly missing a job opportunity due to being unable to retrieve their academic transcript. The university notified government agencies and authorities and worked together to restore the system and make alternative arrangements for the affected students.

3. **Harry Perkins Institute of Medical Research Ransomware (July 2024)**
    - In July 2024, the Medusa ransomware group targeted Harry Perkins Institute of Medical Research, located at Perth. They exfiltrated a large volume of sensitive data including security footages and threatened to publish these data unless a ransom was paid, which the institute refused.
        
        An alleged 4.6TB of data of security footages was reportedly dumped on the dark web as data extortion (where stolen data is publicly released to pressure the victims and damage reputation). The institute later engaged a cyber forensic team to investigate the nature and extent of the published data.
