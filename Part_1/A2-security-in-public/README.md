# A2. Discover security concepts used in public space.

**Overview:** Surveyed public spaces in Perth and train stations identifying different security measures that are used to protect people, property and data.

**Security concepts found:**

1. **CCTV Surveillance**
    - The City of Perth operates a large-scale surveillance network across the CBD. According to their website there are more than 700 cameras that are monitored constantly. CCTV cameras can also be seen in train stations.
2. **Transit Officers and Security Personnel**
    - Transperth has around 400 transit offices across train networks, all wearing boy cameras. WA police also maintain both visible and hidden presence across the CBD. This includes mounted police, bike patrols in areas like Northbridge, as well as unmarked police cars that blend into regular traffic so offenders can never be sure when police are nearby. From time to time, police are seen doing weapons screenings of individuals around higher risk areas like train stations and northbridge.
3. **Help points and Incident Reporting**
    - Help points are located at several bus and train stations, and the Transperth app allows their passengers to discreetly report threatening behaviour in real time, which is especially useful for when speaking out loud isn’t safe. The City of Perth also runs a “See It, Say It” campaign encouraging the public to report anything suspicious, which makes the community as an extra layer of security.
4. **Bollards**
    - Fixed and retractable bollards are present along Murray Street Mall and key pedestrian zones across Perth CBD. These are passive security access control systems designed to stop vehicles from entering pedestrian areas. When up, bollards require no human intervention and provide constant protection to civilians whether security staff are present
5. **Locks and Physical Access Control**
    - Restricted areas across Perth’s public infrastructure like staff rooms, electrical substations and utility rooms are secured with locks or padlocks, preventing access to critical equipment. Even in public space, not all areas are intended to be publicly accessible, and these locks act as the last line of defence when other security measures like officers are not currently present.
